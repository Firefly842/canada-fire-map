About this map

What this map shows

Wildfires reported in Canada, a research model's weekly fire forecasts, the daily Fire Weather Index (FWI), and the conditions behind each fire and forecast, 1 April to 31 October.

How to use it

  • Pick a season and a day (slider, Day, Play; Mon jumps between forecast Mondays), then which fires to show and how to colour them.
  • Shading: the official FWI map, the model's 10 km FWI grid, or the model's chance of a fire.
  • Click a fire, a marked zone or any place for details. The web address keeps your view for sharing.

The model in brief

Every Monday the model estimates, for each 10 km square, the chance that at least one fire of 1 hectare or more will be reported within 28 km in the next 30 days. It averages a decision-tree model and two neural networks. Its two neural networks learned from fire records and weather since 1980, its decision tree since 2015, using station weather and fire weather codes, recent fires nearby, the place's fire history, fuel type and climate indices. Each Monday it marks the 200 zones with the highest chances, with marked centres at least 56 km apart.

How it did

Words used here

Hectare (ha)
An area of 100 m by 100 m (0.01 km²).
Cell
A 10 km by 10 km square of the model's grid. 70,057 cells cover the official FWI map's area: most of Canada, but only a small part of Nunavut.
Zone
The circle of 28 km radius around a cell's centre. Zones of neighbouring cells overlap.
Forecast Monday
The day of the model's weekly forecast.
Counted fire
A fire of at least 1 ha recorded by a fire agency; the model learns from and is scored on these.
Chance
The model's probability that at least one counted fire is reported in the zone in the 30 days after the forecast Monday.
Rank
The zone's place in that Monday's list, highest chance first.
Marked zone
One of the 200 zones the model picks each Monday, highest chance first, with centres at least 56 km apart, so marked zones never overlap: the map's "predicted fires".
Caught fire
A counted fire inside a zone marked on a Monday 1 to 30 days before its report date.
Normal
A cell's 1980-2022 average for the same time of the season.
FFMC
"Fine Fuel Moisture Code (FFMC) is affected by temperature, rain, relative humidity and wind. FFMC represents moisture in small, easily ignitable fuels"
DMC
"Duff Moisture Code (DMC) is influenced by temperature, rain, and relative humidity. DMC tracks moisture levels in decomposing organic matter."
DC
"Drought Code (DC) is affected by temperature and rain. DC measures long-term drying conditions."
ISI
"FFMC and wind influence the Initial Spread Index (ISI), which estimates how quickly a fire will spread."
BUI
"DC and DMC combine to form the Buildup Index (BUI), which indicates the amount of fuel available for a wildland fire to burn."
FWI
"ISI and BUI determine the Fire Weather Index (FWI), which provides an overall measure of fire danger."

Quotations: Natural Resources Canada, "Canadian Forest Fire Weather Index (FWI) System". Higher values mean drier fuels and more danger; colours show the five official classes.

Limits

  • Fires under 1 ha are drawn but not counted.
  • Weather is estimated for each cell from nearby stations; far from a station it is less certain.

    Official information

    Research map, not an official forecast. The fire chances and marked areas on this map come from a statistical model built for research. They are hindcasts: made after the season from information dated up to each Monday, to show how such a model would have done. They can be wrong, and they are not a warning. Do not use this map for safety, evacuation or planning decisions.

    For official and current information, see the Canadian Wildland Fire Information System (https://cwfis.cfs.nrcan.gc.ca), the Canadian Interagency Forest Fire Centre (https://ciffc.ca) and your provincial or territorial wildfire agency. In an emergency, follow the instructions of local authorities.

    This map is not produced, checked or endorsed by Natural Resources Canada, Statistics Canada, the Canadian Interagency Forest Fire Centre, Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada or any fire management agency. Fire records are as reported by the agencies: positions are reported points (not burned areas), dates are report dates (not start dates), and records can be incomplete or revised.
